You think that the loss of human lives is bad.

Let us think about it.

Case 01
Thomas Trader. Age 44. Accountant.

Thomas had a family. A wife. Two children. A house in a suburb that he painted every four years because that was what you did.

He did not like any of them.

Not in a violent way. Not in a way that anyone would have noticed at a dinner table or a school recital. He simply did not feel what he was supposed to feel when he looked at them. He had tried. For a long time he had tried. He went through the motions with the seriousness of someone who understood that the motions mattered even when the feeling behind them was absent.

On the morning of the displacement, Thomas was sitting in his car in the driveway for eleven minutes before going inside. He did this three or four times a week. Just sitting. The engine off. The house lit from inside.

He was, by any honest measure, a man who would have been lighter without the life he was living.

The displacement removed that weight.

The question is whether that is a loss.

Objection

But what about the wife. The two children. The people who were not Thomas. The ones who did feel what they were supposed to feel when they looked at him. Is their loss not the loss you are supposed to be accounting for.
Case 02
Sarah Voss. Age 38. Thomas's wife.

Sarah knew.

Not everything. Not the eleven minutes in the driveway. But she knew the shape of a man who was present without being there, and she had known it for long enough that she had stopped naming it and simply lived alongside it the way you live alongside a sound in the walls that you have decided is not your problem to fix.

She had her own eleven minutes. Hers happened in the bathroom at 6am before the house woke up. Standing at the mirror not looking at anything in particular.

She had a life that looked correct from the outside and felt like wearing the wrong size of everything from the inside.

She was not unhappy in a way she could point to. That was the problem. There was nothing to point to. Just the persistent low feeling of a life that fit the way a jacket fits when someone else bought it for you.

The displacement removed the jacket.

Objection.

You can keep doing this. You can find a reason for every person. That is not a case. That is just looking hard enough at anyone until you find the part of them that was not alright. Everyone has that part.

Yes.

That is precisely the point.

Every person carries a version of Thomas's driveway. A version of Sarah's mirror. A place where the life they are living does not meet the life they are inside. For some it is large and visible. For most it is small and quiet and never spoken about to anyone.

The displacement did not select for the unhappy. It did not sort the worthy from the unworthy. It processed everyone.

But if you look at anyone long enough, with enough honesty, you will find the eleven minutes. The wrong-sized jacket. The thing they were carrying that they would have put down if they had known how.

The argument is not that the loss was good.

The argument is that the loss was already there.

The displacement simply made it visible.

Episode context

03 // THE CASE signifies the collapse of the moral arithmetic. We instinctively count the dead as a total loss. But the cases of Thomas and Sarah force an uncomfortable question: what was being counted? Happiness that never existed? Bonds that held only by inertia? The displacement did not create absence; it revealed the absence that was already there. The argument is not that the loss was justified. The argument is that the accounting was wrong from the start.
